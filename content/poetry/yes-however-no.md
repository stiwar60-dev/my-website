There laid a man once so distraught, so bare;
he held no compassion, no mend to care.
He looked at stars as if they were mere dots in the sky;
you see, he was quite naive,
held damsels in his mind, thought they lied,
white teeth held yellow lies, scars up his bent hind.
Oh, my apologies, he held compassion:
he lacked the quiet lack of it.
He could fuel the streets of Harrenhal with peace,
he could solemnly cure the wrecked trails of grief.
It's a conundrum I was honored to perceive:
he was ridden sick, he harbored the cures,
he was ridden sick, he used to roll up his sleeve.
It’s just that needle wouldn’t go quite in place;
it used to hold, but it used to shake.
What can one do?
A cure doesn’t always heed for immunity;
compassion could be for all the crowds to cheer,
but silently, not for himself withholding his veneer.

And so, the frame quietly altered with time;
the boy grew alongside his mind.
His heart had grown lighter in the meantime,
yet remained so earnest, one might call it divine.
'Tis grand, now, what I write:
the boy held his poor heart in a plight.
I don’t wish to speak too much, just enough,
but could it be said that the boy was in love?
He believed love's the needle; it would relieve,
it wouldn’t hurt, it wouldn’t shake, and it wouldn’t break.
And thus, a question arrived of its own accord:
does love weigh equal in the eyes of all?
They deemed the needle impure,
so how could he be cured?
They said he was ill, but held no name for it,
so he chose to be bedridden and still.
He flipped a coin, prayed for luck;
he felt free, yet lived as though he was stuck.
Oh, my apologies, he wasn’t all that pure:
his eyes had witnessed tumbles no child should endure.
He would call them wounds, but could never trace their blood;
though many storms had passed and many rivers flood,
I would interject, must I say,
tragedies found him night and day.
He appeared unchanged, or so it seemed,
but surely no one remains what they have been,
for who would know the tally of the tragedies he'd seen?
I would call it suffering, though it never agreed to the name;
he appeared unchanged, yet he did not know if he ever felt the same.

And so, the frame didn’t quite alter as much this time;
it was so as the flowers bloom, right before they withered;
it took hours, right before it would take years.
Oh, the boy now held it all so effortlessly,
his smile, his wit, his charm relentlessly.
He had people now, if I remember correctly,
the people who loved him for who he was in the dark;
they would hold his fingers, which held his heart.
Thus, he believed friendship would be the needle;
love had failed him before,
but friendship wore kinder clothes.
It couldn’t rot, it couldn’t be a sin,
it couldn’t turn sour, it wouldn’t hurt within.
This was his turn; this would heal his curse.
Thus, he mistook his for all his universe.
Oh, my apologies, he wasn’t all that effortless;
he never planned it, but it was all so meticulous.
It’s a secret: he held ownership of this blanket.
It never used to curl up on him, it had a habit:
it lay smoothed out, so near to perfection,
but perfection always asks for protection.
And that's what the blanket did in his authority:
it lay over his bugs, his lies, his ruined tragedies.
They were so crude, but it hid them all so beautifully.
If it ever was splashed with blood, it could be cleaned;
it could heal, even if the boy wouldn’t quite see it bleed.
At times, the boy would dream: what if the blanket leaves?
The boy would be shapeless, deemed for invisibility.
It's just that the blanket had seen so many seasons;
it became the sheets, the pillow, and his life’s reason.
You see, I never knew where the blanket came from;
maybe the boy was born with it?
However, that can’t be; it wouldn’t fit his story.
Maybe he had to weave it in secrecy,
so the tragedies he had seen
he could hide underneath.
So much talk of this blanket, it's tiring;
all you need to know, he held close onto it in binding.
His friends, they never saw the blanket as anything more than a blanket;
it wasn’t ignorance, it was more of a habit.
The boy knew they meant well;
they used to tuck him well.
And as time grew and as the winds swayed,
somewhere along the way,
he mistook acceptance for safety.
You see, he grew smaller lately,
but the blanket kept spreading greatly.
The blanket wasn’t overbearing;
it was just too visible to still be caring.
It became the joke; it became all he was known for.
Soon, as you knew, the sheets pulled all over him.
I believe it was the crimes of his friends,
or it could have been his own hands.
Let me think clearly:
this part of the story, memories tend to be blurry.
Ah well, what matters is that he couldn’t quite see;
he couldn’t really breathe.
He had to make a few incisions.
Where? Sadly, I don’t entail that permission.
But his teeth could feel the wind at times;
he could melt in the sun sometimes.
His friends seemed to be his gods, you see;
they used to tuck him well entirely.
Maybe that's why they knew where to pull, sadly:
to strip him of everything, his love, his lies, his innocence.
It all came spurting out; it wasn’t planned, it was a massacre.
He didn’t die, but the wounds they left,
how could he now ever hide?
Were they wounds truly, or just lessons learned?
Oh, but don't you worry, my readers: he was stripped naked,
but then he felt his skin, he felt his need.
But what’s to come next,
he couldn’t quite tell;
he wasn’t really cured still.
It was all just quietly left in the open.
They stripped him of the blanket, and something more besides,
for afterward he could not tell
where the blanket ended
and where the boy resides.
He heard the truths they spoke of him,
yet none would quite amend.
Perhaps he slowly came to think
he was a frame for any friend.
You see,
he slowly became the edge of the bed,
the place where people linger
before they leave instead.
He gave them all he had to give,
yet searched for something more.
That was the tragedy of it:
he lost himself
before he lost them all.

The ripples of woeful wounds didn’t come to an end;
it grew, enraged with ridicule; it soared, my friends.
As the wails of the blanket passed through,
the boy discovered an abstract yet a real pursuit:
the gentle fade through the crevices underneath his painted nails,
the absurdly aggressive tickle in his nostril’s hair.
It’s a marvelous wonder of the seven heavens;
it’s ubiquitous, it’s pretty rare: for the boy, it was deemed air.
Don’t you make deformed eyes at him;
can you blame the poor kid who lay underneath the sheath?
He claimed it as protection, but I wish to say the truth:
he had to grow weary of the blanket.
You see, the blanket wasn’t ever a myth,
but it had lived too many lives to still fit.
And so the boy now emerges
from the harrows of pain
and the wrecked veins of the birches.
It could be said he was free,
but his feeble mind never learned:
never learned how to swim through the rain,
how to sleep without his mother’s embrace,
how to breathe air in and out with grace.
I would like to believe his eyes were mere to his heart;
there seemed to be a void, hollow yet aggressive,
between his syllables and his teeth.
How to overcome such tragedy, you must ask?
There seemed to be this tunnel underneath all the havoc;
it might have been abundant once in someone’s heart,
but as all good turns bitter and sour,
it, too, might have been forgotten.
The boy’s eyes couldn’t make sense of it;
it seemed delicate, but it held admirable space.
Was it a needle? Was it a pill?
I wish I could tell still.
For the tunnel stretched further than his sight allowed;
its walls seemed worn by something older than memory,
and every step within it carried an echo,
as though someone had walked there long before him.
The boy could not tell whether it led somewhere,
or whether it merely wished to be followed.
But this curious object that held the boy entirely,
it was this old narrator of yours, sincerely.
Now you must ask, who am I?
Who am I?
I held the space between the dark and grey,
the space between the hurt and his teeth.
I don’t call myself any remedy;
I wish I knew if I could ever be of use in his tragedies.
But I come with the sharks, and leave with the trees.
I had a mind of his heart,
feet to the steps; I taught him how to breathe calm.
I held his ribs when maybe air became too much to last.
There, the boy stood in the tunnel of some art.
It’s comforting, I believe.
The walls had been carved but were empty presently.
I arrived at the scene, but gone through unnoticed.
I prefer the cracks, you see;
my eyes glance up at them, and I see beauty in this dark:
the pause before a trembling answer,
the silence after a wound is named,
the curious distance
between what one feels
and what one is willing to say.
You could search for me in books
and still miss me entirely.
You could stumble upon me by accident
and never be the same.
Thus, I held my pen and covered the walls,
fueled them with pieces of the boy’s heart;
I did so until he learned to breathe with ease,
even when old wounds whispered underneath.
Thus, the naivety of the boy believed that I was the cure:
the cure after love’s wreath left him beseeched,
the cure after friendship’s grace left him worms for dreams.
However, at least my bedridden eyes had seen it all:
his wounds, his love, his trust, his falls.
But to be cured, that’s a disease I can’t really explore.
His wounds had been fresh for years.
I held a power, you see, that the boy could not perceive:
I had felt the wounds that he had never watched to bleed.
I could not nourish them, nor close them at the seam,
but I traced them for as long as I was permitted to be.
I told him how to turn his wrists inside out,
breathe it all in first, and then let it out.
It looked pretty, the wounds now covered underneath the air so pretty;
he knew now what breathing meant to him and me.
I walked in doors that had been sealed shut part by part,
and watched the waves return to his drought-ridden heart.
I held a mirror to them wounds,
so much crudeness to be had.
I held my hands against them;
it made the boy grow mad.
For every wound appeared far larger
than he believed it to be,
and every scar he thought forgotten
still whispered underneath.
I was never any remedy,
I knew no cure by name,
but I could teach his eyes
to look directly at the pain.
And when at last he saw it,
though it frightened him to death,
the wound no longer hid itself
beneath another breath.
And as the years took a breath reformed,
the boy would feel somehow more and more cured.
He learned the flux of air to be good for him in need,
but as the leaves withered grey,
and as the sun started setting in the east,
more and more wounds came knocking on his door.
Thus, the hinge between me and him started blurring:
more he faced, more I traced,
more he broke, more I wrote.
My hands weren’t made of iron or steel,
but I made sure the wounds had to heal.
Thus, the cracks in the walls covered themselves more and more;
the wrinkled pages over them increased so beautifully.
It became rather a maze, a maze of all his joy and pain.
However, pain has a habit just like knowledge:
it’s meant to be gained, but it slowly, over time, starts to strain.
There arrived a halt in our magic.
Now, when blood knocked on the door,
my eyes knew where the trail would follow.
The boy knew well enough to know it would all be hollow.
The steps that once walked now quietly fell through flat.
Don’t get me wrong, it never was a bad thing;
it’s this mere hint of sorrow that followed me.
I was once an art and its artist;
I learned his sorrow like it was mine.
But if I can name the wound before it arrives,
still can’t seem to prevent the blood’s rise,
then what good am I?
Let the boy breathe:
he was never wise,
he merely learned the language of his sickness too well.
Let me breathe:
I was never a cure,
I merely learned the language of his wounds too well.
And as the rain traced his wounds,
he grew tired to take the oxygen out of the air’s womb.
He knew what his heart would tell him:
I didn’t quite fail,
I surpassed.
And now I see the moment where the boy forgot what air meant to his mind.
The tunnel had overflowed at this sincere point of time.
It wasn’t malice or fueled with roaches;
it was so beautiful, but the boy knew:
the boy knew he couldn’t stand still in the light,
when I made the tunnel for his heart and mind.
But again, I ask:
what was ever the tunnel?
Me, right?
My pieces covered the walls of it.
It was the heart of the boy,
but I held the key to all his myth and joy.
So if I couldn’t seem to break the chain,
the tunnel had to go off its rails.
You see, for years I had held a lantern to his wounds,
and somehow,
without noticing,
the flame had begun consuming me too.
It burned me down, till all there was were mere ashes he could say were pieces of his heart.
Thus, now we entered a chapter where the story seems plain but stark.
I stopped bleeding on the page,
and the boy stopped bleeding within.
The boy agreed to the name of suffering at last,
but he knew it to be fruitless to feel hurt from what’s present or the past.
Thus, I sat there clueless, with my pen knuckled between my toes.
I wish I could say I knew better,
but there came a time
where I seemed to know it all,
and I rose so high where all I could do was fall.
And the boy breathed so well that air wasn’t air at all.

He was normal to the eyes of all, of me and even of you.
He never really quite used to bleed, or think of being.
But you know how, when a shooting star comes to shine,
it shines as it falls in front of our mere eyes.
"But I thought you thought they were mere dots in the sky."
Well, time grew on me, and so my eyes grew on them.
But there’s this intrigue, you see:
how all beings sit around or stand in awe,
cheer if need be,
for a star that slowly falls and falls down its trajectory.
That’s the day it dawned upon me
that maybe sadness in itself is an art that’s cherished,
or at least the one that’s more visible to a naked eye.
And maybe even to me, it’s an art that made me feel closer to what I am supposed to be.
"Thus, I was born of want, and not need?"
Yes, however, no.
Yes, I wished for me to feel what one feels, but without having to face absurd tragedies.
However, most of those tragedies did befall me;
thus, you see, my three wishes came true at the cost of me.
"Were you ever really sick?"
Oh yes, while I started finding happiness in the sadness,
the tragedies became more and more obscene.
The blood had started boiling,
and the wounds had become too close to each other.
And then, came you.
You weren’t dressed in white or silver;
you were in shades of caramel and green.
You held my pen, and you gave me a place to store that sadness in me.
"Don’t you affirm those notions within me!
I held some sunshine, too, with all your rain.
It might have been not as bright,
but it still, at times, used to shine."
I agree. See, I have a habit of being in rain rather than to be seen in the sun.
I was a kid; I wanted sadness until I saw up close what blood feels like.
"Thus, I did my work, part by part.
Couldn’t quite heal, but I gave a pathway for those blots.
You, then, somehow healed?"
Oh no, you had made me see things from a different lens.
I had lived within you for so long
that I started seeing that what I see was wrong.
I wished to spew blood rather than drink peace,
and I had held that needle to cure a wound
that was born from a shooting star one too many times,
and it had broken.
"Out of conscience, or because it could never hold?"
But because the hands it used to hold used to rattle in the heated cold.
"And thus, I had to leave.
Just as the blanket,
I had to leave:
not because I failed,
not because I surpassed,
but because the need I was born for
wasn’t needed anymore.
You saw suffering for suffering at last,
and with all the ink that bled, you finally saw the truth of life:
some wounds come, some wounds ask to leave,
but some, sadly, stick for life.
They sit in a chest so hollow that they feel like they hold value.
And they might as well; however, with time, as you said,
just like knowledge, just like pain, and now just like wounds,
they settle, too.
They don’t heal; they just grew weary to still bleed.
And maybe that’s the way I felt like something unseen,
because my blood could never be traced;
it was just to be felt by you, and you were too fixated on your own to see."
I am sorry, I apologize.
But if you had never been, I would have never finally seen
that all this search for a cure landed with more wounds down the narrow.
If you had never left,
I would've kept looking.
But now I believe I am ready:
I am ready to finally be,
as my life begins to fit my clothes.
As my blanket had burnt and spread within the tunnel,
I finally wish for you to be beside me.
"I am ready for it, too.
I grew weary of bleeding, and I know:
I know there are more wounds to knock at the shores,
more tunnels to walk through,
more blankets to burn right through."
But "we" aren’t "searching" for "a" cure "no" more.
"I wish to sway with the hunches of your soul,
no matter if it’s tiny or admirably sore."
And I wish to sit, when it’s not so warm or so cold;
I think we could call it growth.
"However, calling it for what it is, isn’t that not so wise?"
Yes, however, no.
